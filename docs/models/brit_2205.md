# Brit 2205 (Marshall JCM800 2205, boost channel) research log

Engineering identity: **Marshall JCM800 2205 Split Channel Reverb, 50 W**, the
**boost channel**, in its **later ("post-1984") circuit**, from Marshall's own
1987/88 drawings. Stable id `amp_jcm800_2205` (the `circuit` parameter,
appended). Display: **Brit 2205**. Matched power stage: **Brit 2205 EL34**
(`power_2205_el34`, appended).

Status: **IMPLEMENTED 2026-09-24** (`src/circuits/brit2205.rs`,
`power::PowerSpec::BRIT_2205_EL34`, `Gain::Brit2205`, `Circuit::Brit2205`,
`PowerAmp::Brit2205EL34`), tested in `tests/brit2205.rs`; probe with
`cargo run --release --example brit2205_op`. Asked for because the
*Machine Rage '92* preset "sounds much too clean": that preset is Tom Morello's
rig, which is a 2205's boost channel, and it was standing on the Brit 800 --
the single-channel 2203, which has neither of the 2205 boost channel's two
distinguishing parts.

## Why this amplifier, and why not the 2203 with more gain

The 2203 and the 2205 share a family name and a power-amplifier family and
nothing else in the preamplifier. Marshall's own manual gives the difference as
sensitivity: the 2205's boost channel reaches its **minimum clipping level at
0.4 mV** with the controls full, against the normal channel's 6 mV. The later
boost channel gets there with two things the 2203 does not have:

- **V1B is diode-biased.** A 1N4007 sits across its 10 k cathode resistor, so
  the cathode is clamped near 0.6 V and the diode's small dynamic resistance
  bypasses it: a high-gain stage on a **470 k** plate load.
- **A diode clipper.** Past the second gang of the Gain pot the signal goes
  through 27 k (2 nF across it) into a bridge rectifier with a 1N4007 across its
  DC terminals: three junctions in either direction to ground, about ±1.4 V
  at the currents 27 k allows (`tests/brit2205.rs`).
  This is the "diode clipping" the split-channel JCM800s are known for.

A 2203 turned up is a different amplifier turned up. The preset's own log
already said so ("its extra preamp stage is not modeled").

## Eight-question checkpoint

1. **Revision.** Two boost-channel circuits exist under the one model number,
   and they are genuinely different:
   - **1981-83** ("early"): Marshall's hand-drawn *Model 4210 50 W Reverb Combo --
     Circuit Diagram, Model 2205 50 W amp head*, issues 1-5 (26-10-81 to
     12-9-83). The boost channel's tone stack sits **between** its gain stages,
     a cathode follower (V2b) drives **four 1N4007s** (two in series each way),
     and the master is a dual-gang pot **after** the phase inverter.
   - **late 1984-89** ("later"): Marshall's CAD drawings *2205 Preamp* (issue 1
     6/3/87, issue 2 18-5-88, PCB JM83) and *2205 STD Output Stage & PSU* (issue
     1 12/3/87, issue 2 4-5-88). Tone stack **after** the gain stages, the
     diode-biased V1B, the bridge clipper, and a master **before** the inverter.

   The change reached production in late 1984; 1984 date codes are found with
   either circuit, 1985 on with the later one (WIDELY REPORTED: Marshall Amp
   Forum *JCM800 2205: 1984 vs 1986*; Totally Rad Guitars' teardowns of a 1982
   2210 and a 1985 2205).

   **This model is the later circuit.** Morello bought his amplifier used in
   1988 and it is described as "mid-1980s"; its build date is **not documented**
   in anything located, so which circuit it carries is **PLAUSIBLE, not
   DOCUMENTED**: the later circuit covers roughly five of the model's seven
   production years and is the one Marshall's drawing of record shows.
2. **Original schematic found?** **Yes.** Both manufacturer drawings, from
   [Dr. Tube's Marshall JCM800 page](https://www.drtube.com/marshall-jcm800/):
   - [`2205prem.gif`](https://www.drtube.com/schematics/marshall/2205prem.gif), the preamp, issue 2 18-5-88;
   - [`2205pwrm.gif`](https://www.drtube.com/schematics/marshall/2205pwrm.gif), output stage and PSU, issue 2 4-5-88;
   - [`4210.gif`](https://www.drtube.com/schematics/marshall/4210.gif), the early circuit, issue 5 12-9-83 (context only);
   - [the split-channel owner's manual](https://www.drtube.com/schematics/marshall/jcm800-sc-manual.pdf)
     for the front panel and the specification.

   Local copies in `docs/schematics/marshall_*` (git-ignored). Both CAD sheets
   are legible value by value; the reads below were made at 2.5-5x.
3. **Best source.** The two 1987/88 CAD drawings. They carry reference
   designators, which the code uses.
4. **Cross-check.**
   - The owner's manual's front panel: Normal channel *Volume, Treble, Bass*;
     Boost channel *Gain, Volume, Treble, Middle, Bass*; *Master Reverb, Master
     Volume, Master Presence*. Every one is a pot on the drawing, in that
     arrangement.
   - The manual's specification: boost sensitivity 0.12 mV, minimum clipping
     level 0.4 mV, "Master Volume control operating on both channels but with
     gain priority to boost channel", presence +6 dB at 4 kHz, 50 W into 8 ohm.
   - Neural DSP's user manual for *Archetype: Tom Morello*, which models this
     amplifier: a 2-band Normal EQ, 3-band Boost EQ, and master Presence, Volume
     and Reverb -- the same panel.
   - The **mains transformer part number, 1202-324, is the same** on the 2205's
     1988 PSU sheet and on the 1981 *JCM800 Lead Series 50 W & 100 W Power
     Circuit Diagram*'s 50 W stage (used on the 1987, 2104, 2204), which is how
     the supplies below are anchored.

6. **Exactly modelled.**
   - Every part in the boost channel's signal path above, from the jack to the
     inverter's input, including both gangs of the Gain pot on one knob, the
     diode-biased V1B, the clipper, the plate-to-rail 2 nF on V3A, the stack,
     the master rheostat, the passive mix and V4A.
   - The muted Normal channel's passive network, as the load it is on V1A's
     plate and on G: a real 2205 in boost still has it hanging there.
   - The inverter as drawn: 330 k / 120 k grid leaks, 470 ohm cathode, 22 k +
     4k7 tail, the 0.1 uF cross-coupling, 82 k / 100 k plates, 100 pF across
     them, and 100 k of feedback from the 4 ohm tap with the 22 k presence.
7. **Approximated.**
   - **The clipper.** In either polarity the current goes through two bridge
     diodes and D6, and the two bridge diodes that are not conducting are
     reverse biased. For identical junctions, three Shockley diodes in series
     are **exactly** one diode with three times the emission coefficient, so the
     bridge and D6 are built as an anti-parallel pair of such diodes: two
     nonlinear parts and no internal nodes instead of five and two. The W005's
     cells are taken to be the same 1N4007-class rectifier junction as D6
     (APPROXIMATED: no W005 SPICE data located). `tests/brit2205.rs` builds
     the full bridge beside it and holds the two to the same output.
   - **1N4007** as the widely used SPICE parameters, IS 7.03 nA, N 1.808
     (PUBLISHED-PARAMETER); series resistance (34 mohm) and junction capacitance
     are omitted as everywhere else in the catalogue.
   - **Supplies ESTIMATED.** The 2205 sheets carry no voltages.
     - Plates **460 V**, screens **450 V**: what 50 W JCM800s measure (Ampbooks'
       2204 analysis; owners' readings of 425-475 V by year and transformer),
       WIDELY REPORTED. Marshall's 1981 *50 W & 100 W Power Circuit Diagram*
       prints 365 V for its 50 W stage on the same 1202-324 mains transformer,
       and that figure was tried first: the stage then made **31 W**, against the
       2205's own rating of 70 W at 4 % and the 57 W Ampbooks derives. No
       measured amplifier bears it out, so it is recorded and not used.
     - The inverter/preamp rail X at **365 V**: the screen node less R59
       10 k's drop, with the inverter's 2.47 mA (`examples/brit2205_op.rs`) and
       the preamplifier's ~6 mA through it. The preamplifier's own rail A is
       then set by its current through R58 10 k: **307.5 V**.
     - Bias **-36 V**, which RV1 (a 22 k preset) would be set to for **38 mA**
       and 17 W a valve at idle with the catalogue's EL34 fit -- the quantity a
       technician sets is the current, and 32-40 mA is the JCM800 range.
     - The unbuilt valves on A (the muted Normal channel's V2 pair and the
       reverb recovery V4B, about 2 mA) are a 120 k bleed from A.
     - The one published reading of this preamp agrees: an owner's 2210
       notes give V1B **83 V** plate and **0.52 V** cathode; the model gives
       86.8 V and 0.51 V (a second owner's 2205 read 60 V and 0.5 V).
   - **Output transformer: replacement-part data.** 789-139's data is not
     public. The Hammond **1750N** is sold as the 50 W JMP/JCM800 drop-in and
     its datasheet gives 3.2 k plate to plate, 41.74/43.14 ohm half primaries,
     18.3 H and 13.41 mH at 1 kHz (PUBLISHED-PARAMETER DERIVED, as the Brit
     EL34 stage uses the 1750U for the 2203). The core is sized to just hold
     50 W at 70 Hz, as that stage's is for 100 W.
   - **Not modelled:** the Normal channel as a playable channel (the model is
     the boost channel, which is the only one Morello uses); the reverb (tank,
     driver V3B and recovery V4B -- the send and return are present only as the
     loads they are, with Reverb at zero); the effects loop (its normally
     closed contact is closed); the DI output; the channel-switching IC and
     transistors (fixed in the boost state); heater and mains effects.
   - **Controls without a panel knob.** The boost channel's own Volume (VR5)
     rests at **0.6**, Morello's reported setting (below). The Drive knob is
     Gain, the Master knob is Master Volume, the tone knobs are the boost
     channel's Bass, Middle and Treble. Presence (VR11) rests at the middle
     with the power stage: the plugin has no Presence parameter for any
     amplifier, so Morello's reported 7 cannot be set.
   - **The hand-off.** The preamplifier ends at V4A's plate through a 10 uF
     block that is not on the drawing, loaded by 250 k (R41's 330 k in
     parallel with the power netlist's open 1 M master track), and the power
     netlist takes it through the project's usual 10 k source. C25, the
     drawing's coupling capacitor, stays in the power stage, so any other
     preamplifier played into the Brit 2205 EL34 gets it too.
8. **Why.** The later circuit is the one the manufacturer drew legibly and the
   majority of production. Everything electrically in the boost channel's path
   is built; what is left out is either switched out in boost (the Normal
   channel's valves), not part of the dry sound (reverb, loop, DI), or not
   published (iron, rails), and the last are anchored to Marshall's own figures
   for the sibling amplifier on the same mains transformer.
